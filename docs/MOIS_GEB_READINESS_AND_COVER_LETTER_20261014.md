Working title: **The geographic and measurement dependence of flower-colour–moisture associations across a photographed global flora**

**Claim to publish:** Across 20,694 photographed flowering-plant species and 1,934 sampled genera, the relative abundance of photographed-white flower colour increases with rainfall (+2.255 pp per original source SD, source 24-maxT95 [+0.319,+4.190]) and atmospheric vapour (+3.952 [+1.199,+6.704]) in geographically adjusted **congeneric species comparisons**. White–moisture gradients are stronger between genus means (annual rainfall +8.555) and vary in sign among genera, rather than demonstrating stable genetic morph frequencies. On the **same** species, exploratory altitude- and geography-adjusted joint rain/vapour fitting retains positive individual points but neither individual 2-axis simultaneous interval excludes zero; a common standardized rainfall+vapour direction is positive **+3.644 pp [two-contrast simultaneous95 +1.137,+6.152]**. The latter is an exploratory reparameterization of observed climate variables, **not a measured plant water supply or causal pigment effect**. Strict-quality, local geographic support, independent botany and within-species photographic checks continue to limit universal generalization. The contribution is a source-backed taxonomic-scale and moisture-related macroecological pattern, not relative floral pigmentation fitness or balancing selection.

**Novelty against verified precedents:** Dalrymple et al. (2020; DOI 10.1111/nph.16737) already found precipitation/other ecological correlations in 339 species; Trethowan et al. (2024; DOI 10.1111/jbi.14751) already found white flowers common in wet lowland Malesian environments. Accordingly, a result framed merely as "white in wet places" would be incremental. The potential contribution here is a much broader original species-conditional photographic comparison with **matched support, measurement nonresponse, multiple-comparison and transfer diagnostics** that show precisely where global slopes can and cannot be generalized. This is *not* a confirmed physiological mechanism or demonstration that photo quality causes the broad association.
