# Are white and coloured flowers globally balanced? Ecological hypotheses, original-source constraints and tests

**Research note — 14 October 2026.** This note extends the *interpretation and falsifiable hypotheses* of the global interspecific `mois` work. It is **not a test confirming a pigment trade-off or balancing selection**. The primary submission remains a photographic trait–environment association paper. Newly computed arithmetic is fully reproducible from immutable original FCP aggregate receipts using `scripts/analysis/mois_original_photo_white_balance_20261014.py`. The new receipt is `results/mois_original_photo_white_balance_20261014.json`.

## 1. Three different questions must not be conflated

- **Photo balance:** Are the *classifiable photographed flower-colour labels*, counting each original unique photo opportunity equally, 50% white and 50% nonwhite? This is observable descriptively, conditional on the colour classifier.
- **Floristic or species balance:** Is the white category found in 50% of all flowering-plant *species*, equally weighted across species/genera, communities or independently sampled biogeographic regions? This is **not** the same estimator. The 42,111 nominal source taxa include unclassifiable images, and the all-photo union deliberately combines one-photo-per-taxon with additional taxon×cell opportunities. A species-level white/nonwhite census cannot be inferred from its aggregate photo count; a new original-ZIP replay does estimate an *observed-classifiable-taxon photographic average*, not the unknown all-flora species ratio.
- **Stable evolutionary polymorphism:** Are heritable white and coloured *morphs within a population* maintained by frequency-dependent, temporally varying or spatially antagonistic natural selection? Between-species 50:50, even if exact and representative, does not imply within-population polymorphism, stable genotype frequencies, equal lifetime fitness, or any cost-benefit crossing.

## 2. Results actually present in the source (not newly sampled)

The source union has **100,543 unique photos** from **42,111 nominal taxa**. Only **45,953** receive one of four visible photo-hue labels, of which **21,574 white (46.94797%)** and **24,379 coloured (53.05203%)**. Thus the classified photo ratio is **not exactly 50:50**. Equal counted shares would require re-labelling at least **1,403 classified coloured photos as white**, an arithmetic sensitivity not evidence of 1,403 errors. Source: `results/source_receipts/global_classified_white_fraction_and_compensation.json`.

**54,590 photos are unclassifiable.** If every one had a biologically meaningful but hidden binary white/nonwhite identity, the possible white share of all 100,543 original opportunities would be **21.4575–75.7527%**. An *exact hypothetical* 50% would require **52.57% of unclassified photos to be white**, slightly higher than the observed fraction among classifiable photos. The unknown true white share is not estimated by this hypothetical equation; some photos might not contain a diagnosable flower to assign a latent class. Original source photo selection was nonrandom, and changing the definition from photo to species, individual, or genotype also changes the target population.

The classified photo-white fraction by *absolute source photo-site latitude* was **47.08% (0–30°, n=19,642)**, **46.79% (30–60°, n=25,033)**, and **49.00% (60–90°, n=1,204)** among 45,879 georeferenced classifiable photos. The latitude-group observed range (**2.2131 percentage points**) was **not unusually even** under within-species original colour-label shuffling (999 draws; lower-range one-sided `P=0.214`). A distinct within-genus conditional null gave `P=0.053`; this was one of several historical analyses and does not establish active homeostasis or a 50:50 target.

The source's explicit **geographic compensation** decomposition included 29,117 classified photo observations across 96 evaluable geographic cells. The covariance between the species-composition and within-species-local components was **positive, +0.0001814445** (correlation +0.241), not the *negative* covariance expected under a compensatory cancellation interpretation; one-sided permutational `P=0.997` for negative compensation. The exploratory positive co-clustering was unstable with higher source-photo-depth species gates. **No balancing mechanism is identified.**

## New real-source taxon-weighted result — observational, not fitness (14 October 2026)

An independently executed replay on the **exact checksum-verified original photographic source ZIP** now extends the earlier aggregate-only 46.95% statistic: **25,064 taxa** had ≥1 classified photo, giving a **species-equal white proportion of 46.819%**; averaging taxon photo-white fractions first within and then equally across **5,369 nominal genera** gave **49.891%**, with a matched **genus–species gap +3.072 percentage points [95% genus bootstrap +1.630,+4.602]**. This is an **observational weighting contrast**, not evidence of opposing pigment costs and pollination benefits. **2,529 genera were represented by only one classifiable species**; the genus-equal estimate falls to **46.626%** when genera require at least five such species, undercutting a simple fixed global half-white target.

Among the original **42,111 nominal source taxa**, **17,047 lacked any classified photo**. The hypothetical full-taxon mean white-label probability has no-assumption bounds **27.866–68.347%**; for it to be exactly 50%, taxa with no classifiable photo would need to average **54.677%** latent white. These are not biological fitness observations. The full source-derived dataset, methods, bootstrap, ecological caveats and exact original-ZIP replayer are in `docs/MOIS_SOURCE_REPLAYED_SPECIES_GENUS_WHITE_BALANCE_20261014.md` and `results/mois_original_photo_species_genus_balance_20261014.json`; original photo and source-position rows remain outside public mois.

## 3. What the macroecology actually implies

The GEB manuscript's genus-equal global source analysis (20,694 species, 1,934 genera) reports white/BIO12 **+2.255 percentage points/source SD** and white/water-vapour **+3.952**, with within-scope maxT 95% limits excluding zero. This is a **between-species photographed colour–environment association**, plausibly consistent with habitat-associated sorting or lineage turnover. Its sum-to-one photo-colour classes mechanically give a compensating *total* coloured-category slope. **That compositional arithmetic is not a resource, physiological or lifetime-fitness trade-off.**

Matched source photos (21,448 images, 6,143 named species) show positive between-congeneric rain–white slope (+2.722 points/source SD) but no supported shared positive within-species slope (−0.511, CI −1.436 to +0.335). A genus×spatial-cell analysis does not supply the missing within-population fitness information. Neither makes pollen transfer, UV physiology, clonal growth, longevity, or pigment production cost observable.

## 4. Competing biological models worth actually testing

**H1 — Environmental fitness crossing:** A pigmented form might gain stress protection, thermal or pollinator advantages in dry/high-light habitats, while an unpigmented form might gain resource allocation or pollination benefits in other conditions. The predicted test is a **morph × imposed environment interaction in lifetime relative fitness**, ideally changing sign across well-replicated water/UV/pollinator treatments. *White is not synonymous with zero flavonoid synthesis or zero metabolic expense*: invisible UV-absorbing compounds are common in visibly white flowers. The source BIO12 slope alone is not an estimate of this interaction.

**H2 — Negative frequency-dependent selection (rare-morph advantage):** Within a *truly polymorphic population*, a morph's **per capita reproductive success** may rise when it is experimentally rare. Let `p` be the white *individual/genotype frequency* (not fraction of sampled photos), `W_W(p)` and `W_C(p)` their lifetime relative fitnesses. In a simple haploid-like two-phenotype approximation, `Δp = p(1-p)(W_W−W_C)/Wbar`. A stable internal equilibrium needs `W_W(p*)=W_C(p*)` with white fitter below `p*` and coloured fitter above. `p*=0.5` requires additional symmetry, and is **not** predicted by balancing selection in general. Use replicated floral arrays at known **10%, 30%, 50%, 70%, 90% white frequencies** plus visitation, conspecific pollen deposition, pollen export, seed success and survival; compare frequency slopes, not counts alone. Rewarding pollination can favour common phenotypes, so rare-morph advantage cannot be assumed from pollinator presence.

**H3 — Conflict across life stages:** A floral signal may increase pollen receipt/export but be correlated with vegetative anthocyanin, leaf stress tolerance, herbivore avoidance, delayed flowering or clonal growth. Identify **seedling survival × phenotype**, **reproduction × phenotype**, and **their product across full life stages**. Without genotypes, within-family or near-isogenic comparisons, linkage and species-history effects remain confounders.

**H4 — Nonadaptive compositional appearance:** Species turnover, phylogenetic conservation, uneven flower-photo classification, photography propensity and unequal photo sampling may yield a near-half worldwide photographic category without frequency-dependent selection. This is a serious alternative, not a nuisance to remove until the ecology appears significant.

## 5. Decision-oriented empirical and analysis plan

1. **Immediate aggregate/source-level check (done):** Reproduce original photo denominators and 50% tipping assumption; show latitude-classified fractions; retain both source shuffle-null results and the negative-compensation failure. Do not manufacture a species-equal estimate from photo totals. New executable/receipt committed.
2. **Macroecological source analysis (partly done):** The exact original ZIP replay now reports the breadth one-photo-per-taxon, species-equal, genus-equal and minimum-classifiable-species-per-genus results, with original whole-genus bootstrap uncertainty and missing-taxon bounds. **Not done:** independently authenticated colour, phylogenetically resolved clade/continent/biome comparisons, representative floristic sampling or adjusted genus coverage models. None supplies pigment chemistry or fitness.
3. **Selective-agent prediction (not identified by current receipts):** Fit independent plant morphology, habitat drought/high irradiance and pollinator guild data at species level, including shared genus/clade and biogeographic effects, *without labelling predictors as direct costs or net reproductive benefits*. Evaluate exposure×pollinator interactions rather than only a climatic main effect.
4. **Fitness experiment (genuine test):** In one or more genetically verified, naturally polymorphic species, replicate populations, genotype families/clones and field water regimes; measure VIS/UV reflectance and floral/vegetative pigments, pollen export, pollen receipt, seed quantity/viability, growth and survival. Factorially manipulate relative flower morph frequencies and pollinator access. Model `log(W_white/W_coloured) = α + β_water·water + β_UV·UV + β_pollinator·pollinators + β_freq·p_white + interactions + random(population,genotype,year)`. Test `β_freq < 0` and interior sign-crossing separately from any `morph×water` fitness crossover; calculate `p*` with uncertainty. Follow morph frequencies through multiple seasons, including demographic and seed-bank effects.

## 6. Literature relevant to the mechanism (not new data from mois)

- **Gigord, Macnair & Smithson (2001).** Negative frequency-dependent selection in deceptive *Dactylorhiza sambucina* orchids. *PNAS*. DOI: [10.1073/pnas.111162598](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.111162598). Direct precedent for rare-morph benefit, not a universal flower-colour result.
- **Tenhumberg, Dellinger & Smith (2023).** Integrative model combining pollinator-mediated floral selection with vegetative survival selection. *Journal of Ecology*. DOI: [10.1111/1365-2745.14057](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2745.14057). This is a model illustrating which fitness components are needed.
- **Twyford et al. (2018).** Loss of anthocyanin in natural *Mimulus guttatus*, genotype and reproductive/life-history differences; a high-frequency variant did not yield a simple demonstrated cost advantage. *American Naturalist*. DOI: [10.1086/694853](https://doi.org/10.1086/694853).
- **Narbona et al. (2025).** Pigment analysis of 926 animal-pollinated species finds ubiquitous UV-absorbing phenylpropanoids; **visibly white is not simply biologically pigment-free**. *Scientific Reports*. DOI: [10.1038/s41598-025-94709-4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-94709-4).
- **Clegg & Durbin (2000).** *Ipomoea purpurea* colour polymorphisms, genotype effects, pollinator preference and complex opposing selection; strong example of why phenotype ratio alone cannot establish a mechanism. *PNAS*. DOI: [10.1073/pnas.97.13.7016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.97.13.7016).

**Scientific decision:** The positive moist-climate association is a justified GEB-scale *result*; globally stabilized 50:50 photo colour and pigment cost/benefit are **interesting testable hypotheses**, but currently **not evidence-supported additions to that primary conclusion**. The strongest next inference requires truly species-/individual-level denominators and independently measured fitness, not further re-analysis of the same unclassifiable photo labels.
