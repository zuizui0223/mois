Dear Editors of *Global Ecology and Biogeography*,

Please consider our Research Article, **“The geographic and measurement dependence of flower-colour–moisture associations across a photographed global flora.”**

Our study asks **at what taxonomic and observational scales global floral colour–climate associations can be generalized**. Worldwide flower-colour associations with aridity and other abiotic stressors are already documented, including by Dellinger et al. (2026); we do not claim first discovery of a wet–white gradient. From 20,694 photographed flowering-plant taxa in 1,934 sampled genera, we found positive genus-centred white–rainfall and water-vapour associations, but the white–rainfall gradient between genus means was considerably larger than that among congeneric species. In a separate 16,801-taxon soil-complete subset, geography/climate/soil explained only 0.6202% of congeneric photographic-white variation, while adding rainfall/vapour to geography reduced heldout new-genus *congeneric pairwise* squared error by just 0.21% on the original folds (95% interval includes zero). We independently reconstructed historical source estimates and contrasted photo-quality, local-geographic and taxonomic-transfer limits. Importantly, 54.3% of original photographic opportunities could not be classified, and source-conditional near-half white prevalence does not establish balanced genetic morphs, pigment costs or natural selection. Our contribution is therefore a **scale- and measurement-conditional ecological interpretation**, not a claimed universal colour rule.

The closely related high-depth **within-species** study is separate and does not duplicate the genus-level moisture coefficient or this paper's main inference; some original photographic opportunities and measurement infrastructure overlap. We will supply a transparent source-provenance and data/code availability statement with an anonymous reviewer-accessible archived dataset before submission.
