Dear Editors of *Global Ecology and Biogeography*,

Please consider our Research Article, **“The geographic and measurement dependence of flower-colour–moisture associations across a photographed global flora.”**

Our study addresses a general macroecological question: **when do broad species-level trait–environment associations remain interpretable after observational support and biological sampling grain change?** We analysed colour-classified global floral photographs for 20,694 species in 1,934 congeneric comparison groups. White-labelled flowers were associated with higher rainfall and atmospheric vapour pressure under broad geographic adjustment, but comparable directions were not established in stringent photo-quality and genus-by-coarse-cell comparisons. Holding the original species fixed showed that geographic statistical conditioning itself changes the estimate, while an identical-21,448-photo analysis did not support the same positive rainfall–white slope within named species. Out-of-region prediction and independently described botanical hues further constrained portability. Prior studies have already described flower-colour correlations with moisture; our contribution is not rediscovering that correlation, but **quantifying the taxonomic, geographic, measurement and inferential conditions under which such broad signals can—and cannot—be generalized**. These findings inform macroecological analyses of any visually assessed trait drawn from heterogeneous community-science data, without claiming causal pigment investment or floral morph fitness.

The closely related high-depth **within-species** study is separate and does not duplicate the genus-level moisture coefficient or this paper's main inference; some original photographic opportunities and measurement infrastructure overlap. We will supply a transparent source-provenance and data/code availability statement with an anonymous reviewer-accessible archived dataset before submission.
