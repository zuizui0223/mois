Dear Editors of *Global Ecology and Biogeography*,

Please consider our Research Article, **“The geographic and measurement dependence of flower-colour–moisture associations across a photographed global flora.”**

Our study addresses a macroecological question: **how does floral colour covary with water-related climate across taxonomic scales?** In 20,694 flowering-plant species from 1,934 sampled genera, photographed white was positively associated with rainfall and atmospheric vapour among congeners; associations between genus means were stronger. A global near-half of photographed white flowers masked substantial heterogeneity among genera rather than demonstrating a maintained polymorphism. A new analysis on **exactly the same species** showed a positive common precipitation–vapour gradient after geographic and elevational adjustment, whereas the two individual moisture coefficients could not be confidently distinguished in the altitude-adjusted joint model. Image-quality, local spatial and within-species comparisons define limits on extrapolation. Beyond an already recognized wet-region/flower-colour correlation, our contribution is to separate **floristic turnover, congeneric species differences and moisture-related environmental variation** while explicitly accounting for observational sampling. These results provide a framework for studying macroecological trait–environment associations without inferring pigment fitness costs or natural selection from photographs alone.

The closely related high-depth **within-species** study is separate and does not duplicate the genus-level moisture coefficient or this paper's main inference; some original photographic opportunities and measurement infrastructure overlap. We will supply a transparent source-provenance and data/code availability statement with an anonymous reviewer-accessible archived dataset before submission.
