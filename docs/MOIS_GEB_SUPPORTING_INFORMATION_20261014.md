### S2.3 | Is globally photographed white exactly one half? Source-only evaluation

The original photo union classified **21,574 white and 24,379 nonwhite** photo opportunities: white **46.94797%**, coloured **53.05203%**. This is *not exact 50:50*; the numerator counts photos, not equally weighted taxa, genera, individuals or known genetic morphs. The two source photo programmes have different photographic opportunities, so a taxon-level floristic white fraction is not available from these aggregate counts alone. Under an explicitly hypothetical latent white/nonwhite assignment of all **54,590 unclassifiable photos**, an exactly half-white union would require **52.57% of the unclassified photos** to be white; the full-photo logically possible range is **21.46–75.75%**. Neither the true unseen-photo outcomes nor genuine genetic white frequencies are observed.

Within the 45,879 classifiable geotagged photographs, absolute latitude 0–30°, 30–60° and 60–90° recorded white **47.08%, 46.79%, 49.00%**, respectively. Their **2.213 pp** range was not unusually small under the original within-species label-shuffling conditional null (999 permutations, one-sided `P=0.214`); the distinct genus-preserving `P=0.053` is a retrospectively examined null rather than independent evidence of 50:50 regulation. The source spatial-composition decomposition yielded **positive covariance +0.0001814445**, contrary to predicted negative cancellation of between-species composition and within-species local colour shifts (negative-compensation one-sided **`P=0.997`**). These estimates therefore provide **no demonstrated geographic compensation, stable coexistence or frequency-dependent fitness equilibrium**.

### S2.5 | Photo-white genus clustering: does a global near-half imply within-lineage balance?

We **reopened the exact original source photos** and used the same photo-ID deduplicated, previously classified four-hue labels and taxon names. For every nominal species with ≥1 classifiable photo, `p_i` is its mean original photo-white indicator, treating yellow/orange, red/pink and blue/purple together as photographed nonwhite. Unlike the weighting in S2.4, we now test **whether within-genus taxon values are more similar than expected if the same taxa were randomly reassigned across size-preserving nominal genera**. A genus with ≥3 classifiable species is eligible, but neither the true number of species in that genus nor a species phylogeny is estimated.

For the **20,725 classifiable taxa in 1,935 such genera**, define `genus R²` as between-genus taxon-count-weighted sum of squares divided by total between-taxon sum of squares in `p_i`. This is an observational ANOVA-style *clustering statistic*, not a phylogenetic trait-evolution parameter or evidence that a colour fraction is adaptive.

### S2.7 | How widespread are positive congeneric colour–moisture contributions among genera?

We decomposed the **original worldwide genus-centred, genus-equal, broadly geographically adjusted source coefficient** on its unchanged **20,694 qualified taxa /1,934 nominal genera** into signed genus-level cross-product *contributions*, rather than fitting a separate unstable selection model for every genus. This analysis reuses the two original SHA256-verified photo/environment archives with no recolouring, no novel biological samples and no raw per-genus rows publicly released.

Let `p_i` be the classifiable-photograph white fraction of species `i`. Centre `p_i`, environmental axis `x_i` and the source's **absolute latitude, sine longitude and cosine longitude** within each genus. Residualize `x_i` against the genus-centred geography variables using the **global genus-equal regression** (taxon weight `1/n_g`). The Frisch–Waugh–Lovell identity gives the overall moisture coefficient as the ratio of a sum of signed genus numerators to a sum of genus denominators:

```text
c_g = sum_{i in genus g} (1/n_g) * x_residual_i * (p_i - genus_mean_p)
d_g = sum_{i in genus g} (1/n_g) * x_residual_i^2

original genus-equal adjusted slope = sum_g c_g / sum_g d_g
```

A genus with no classified-photo-white variation has **exactly zero** numerator, even if the moisture and geography of its species vary. The `c_g` sign is **not** a genus-specific significant slope, biological within-genus selection coefficient or predictor of its realised lifetime fitness.

On the principal ≥3-classifiable-species-per-genus cohort, **385 genera have no source photo-white variation**; the other **1,549** have some. A new exploratory null shuffles the *species-level source photo-white fractions* **only within each named genus** while keeping original climate/geographic species values, genus membership, sampled taxon counts and the genus photo-white distribution fixed. The source species photographic depth remains recorded but the pairing between photo-white fraction and photo depth is not otherwise conditioned on. There were **1,999 permutations** per taxonomic support scope with one shared set for the two water axes. Original source standard deviations and geographic projections are fixed for each scope. The permutation comparison is **conditional on within-genus exchangeability**, not a universal phylogenetic or photographic-missingness correction.

| Original globally adjusted source axis | Positive genus contributions | Negative contributions | Zero-photo-white-variation genera | Positive among 1,549 photo-white-variable genera | Permutation null mean [95% range] | Exploratory positive-fraction upper-tail *P* |

| Mean annual atmospheric vapour pressure | **841** | 708 | 385 | **54.29%** | 49.66% [47.19%,52.23%] | **0.0005** |

The numerator-weighted global coefficients are **+2.254545 pp/source SD for BIO12** and **+3.951850 for vapour**, agreeing with the pre-existing world climate26 source values to the shown precision. These coefficients reflect the *net* of differently signed genus cross-products: only **42.92%** (830/1,934) and **43.49%** (841/1,934) of all main-scope genera have a positive contribution, respectively. ### S2.10 | Why altitude and latitude slopes change when water-vapour exposure is included

The S2.9 geography-only and geography-plus-water-vapour fits used the same **20,694 climate26-qualified species from 1,934 genera** and the original source photo-colour labels. The difference is the covariate set, not a replacement of the species sample. To determine **why** the geographic coefficients shift, we reloaded both SHA256-verified original photo/environment ZIPs and independently reconstructed the original genus-equal white/BIO12 coefficient **+2.254545 pp/source SD**. Original sampled taxon/photo denominators (**38,498** classified original photographs in the main genus-qualified panel) remained unchanged.

Let `Y` denote the original species-level classified-photo white proportion, `G` the genus-centred original geographic variables (absolute latitude, longitude sine/cosine and elevation), `V` the genus-centred atmospheric-vapour exposure and `W` equal total weights per sampled nominal genus. Weighted least squares gives the *exact omitted-variable coefficient identity*:

```text
beta(G without V) = beta(G given V) + beta(V given G) * gamma(V on G)
gamma(V on G) = (G' W G)^(-1) G' W V
```

The same identity is repeated with BIO12 rainfall included among `G`. All terms use the same species, source-site environmental normalization and broad within-genus geographical conditioning. This algebra holds without a biological causal model and does **not** identify water vapour as an ecological mediator.

| Same-species comparison (white fraction pp) | Model omitting vapour | Model including vapour | Paired change after including vapour [ordinary 95%] |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Absolute latitude, per **+10°**, geography alone | **+0.483** | **+3.083** | **+2.600 [+0.995,+4.233]** |
| Elevation, per **+1,000 m**, geography alone | **−1.903** | **+1.011** | **+2.914 [+1.110,+4.759]** |
| Absolute latitude, per **+10°**, geography+BIO12 | **+1.101** | **+2.830** | **+1.729 [+0.127,+3.370]** |
| Elevation, per **+1,000 m**, geography+BIO12 | **−1.564** | **+0.483** | **+2.047 [+0.148,+3.986]** |

The *point* increase in each row is identical (numerical discrepancy <1×10⁻⁹ percentage points before display rounding) to the negative of the vapour-white coefficient times the regression of vapour on already included geographic/rainfall covariates. Under geography alone the source vapour-white partial coefficient is **+5.106 pp/source SD**, and the fitted vapour-versus-standardized-absolute-latitude and vapour-versus-standardized-elevation coefficients, conditional on the remaining geographic covariates, are **−0.786 and −0.505**. This explains the *statistical* sign changes, not their biological cause.

Because geography and atmospheric vapour are closely related among the original photographed taxa, the genus-equal within-genus source model explains **74.53%** of within-genus source vapour variation using latitude, circular longitude and elevation alone (**VIF 3.927** for adding vapour). The reported intervals are **ordinary, retrospectively constructed paired within-design percentile 95% ranges**, *not* within-model geo4 maxT intervals, or a correction for trying multiple geographically adjusted models. The confidence interval of a **coefficient shift** must not be read as demonstrating causal mediation, altitude-selected pigment benefit, independent latitudinal adaptation or zero residual geographic effects. Within-model original geo4 maxT intervals remain as reported in S2.9.

Once the exposure set includes atmospheric vapour, the signed elevation result is not stable; meanwhile the positive high-absolute-latitude conditional coefficient becomes larger. The source still measures neither flowering-plant demographic prevalence nor anthocyanin costs, seed/pollen relative fitness, colour-morph genotype frequencies or a maintained 50:50 equilibrium.

### S2.17 | Source-independent test-genus white mean: predicting differences between two unseen congeneric taxa

**A distinct prediction target, not another photograph recolouring.** The prior genus-centred unseen-genus regression (S2.16) evaluated each species' photo-white fraction minus the mean of its *test genus*, a retrospective response not available for forecasting the unknown absolute white fraction of a new genus. To test how far the **congeneric colour–environment slope itself** transfers, we construct a *pairwise difference* target on the **same 16,801 original soil36-complete photographed taxa, 1,665 nominal genera and 26,163 original classifiable photos**. Both original source ZIP SHA256 hashes, original photo-ID deduplication and the source hue classifier were independently rechecked. All genera contain at least three qualified source taxa.

For test congeners `i` and `j`, predict `white_photo_fraction_i − white_photo_fraction_j` by the estimated difference of their site-mean environmental predictor vectors: `(x_i − x_j)β`. **No test-genus mean phenotype and no test photocolour label enter this prediction.** The two real taxon white-photo fractions are used **afterward** to score the predicted difference; the task still requires both congeners' environmental site covariates. The training genera alone supply the within-genus-centred photo-white outcomes and genus-equal, zero-intercept `ridge α=0.01` coefficient. Training-fold-only environmental normalization prevents test-fold scaling leakage. Each of five deterministic whole-genus cross-validation folds contains only genera absent from training. Three additional salted SHA256 fivefold genus assignments on the same source species check sensitivity to the holdout partition.

The squared error of the difference is averaged over **all unordered sampled species pairs within each heldout genus**, and then each genus contributes one equal weight. The explicit pairwise calculation equals the computationally efficient identity

```text
mean_{i<j} ((y_i − y_j) − (ŷ_i − ŷ_j))²
  = (2*n_g/(n_g−1)) * mean_i ((e_i − mean_g(e))²)
where e_i = y_i − ŷ_i .
```

This identity was independently checked on synthetic all-pairs data. The response mean `mean_g(y)` is **not** an input for generating pairwise predictions; averaging the **already observed test residuals** occurs only in the scoring identity. Thus this is a directly interpretable contrast forecast rather than one that assumes a new genus's true white mean is known.

| Original SHA256 | **0.316285** | **+0.000665 [−0.000227,+0.001506]** | **−0.000205 [−0.000803,+0.000356]** |

### S6.6 | Independent public iNaturalist flowering annotation availability (real count study; *not* original Mois photo cohort)

On 16 October 2026, a deliberately bounded independent iNaturalist **public platform annotation-count** study queried the 2024–2025 **observation-date** records through [the official observation API](https://www.inaturalist.org/pages/api+reference). Only photographed Plantae observations (taxon ID 47126, photos=true) were included. Five independent count-only requests per cohort tallied total photo-backed observations, any Flowers and Fruits annotation (term ID 12), specifically Flowers (value 13), explicitly No Flowers or Fruits (value 21) and the without-term-12 filter. The 35 requests used one observation per response and were sequentially paced at no faster than one every 1.35 seconds. The full [35-query source receipt with individual request URLs and original response SHA256 hashes](../results/mois_real_inat_photo_phenology_annotation_count_pilot_20261016.json), [retrieval code](../scripts/analysis/mois_real_inat_phenology_count_bias_pilot_20261016.py), and [actual successful source run](https://github.com/zuizui0223/mois/actions/runs/38843210801) are public. The global API also counted **424,670 (1.49%)** explicit No Flowers or Fruits observations, versus **26,015,038 (~91.07%)** observations returned under the without-phenology-annotation filter. These categories are **not synonyms**: an observation lacking Flowers annotation is not known to be biologically nonflowering, nor does Flowers status certify all attached photos contain a classifiable corolla. The seven cohorts cover fixed descriptive geographical boxes and observation dates, not probability-selected original flora sites. Annotation availability depends jointly on species composition, geographic and seasonal observation effort, source media, and user annotation behaviour; the relative contributions are unidentifiable from these aggregate counts alone.

**Non-atomic query limitation:** the five provider total-results searches were not one frozen transaction. Globally, with-annotation plus without-annotation counts summed to **28,565,267**, **617 fewer** than the all-plant-photo count of 28,565,884; smaller nonzero discrepancies (1–14) occurred in five of six regional cohorts. These were retained as source query-mismatch diagnostics, not filled by assigning unknown annotations or floristic outcomes.

This **28.6-million OBSERVATION** external API cohort is *not* the independently frozen FCP original **93,209,351 PHOTO opportunities** from its dated September 27, 2026 snapshot. It covers ALL photo-backed Plantae observations on the current public platform, not exclusively the predefined 42,111 original named taxa. It is therefore a **real observational ascertainment diagnostic**, not an estimate of original Mois photo availability, annotated photograph eligibility, a 13-variable colour–climate effect or white-flower prevalence. The prospective study must retain the entire original colour-blind photo-ID cohort, verify positive phenology annotations with original sampled-image ROI, preserve unannotated and not-looked-up status separately, compare annotation/ROI success by observer, site and local season, and keep Flowers-restricted analysis as a distinctly selected sensitivity.
