# External ecological benchmark: flower-colour classification and moisture before claiming novelty

**15 October 2026: source-independent trait-label analysis completed from a previously saved copy of the released Dryad CSV.** The global water–white regression BIO12 **was not replicated** on independent climate data. It protects the central Mois ecological inference from both exaggerated novelty and a spurious worldwide 50:50 fitness interpretation.

## The substantive, already published external result

**Dellinger, Meier, Smith & Sinnott-Armstrong (2026), _American Journal of Botany_ 113(1): e70044, DOI [10.1002/ajb2.70044](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajb2.70044)** (first online publication **14 May 2025**). This team analysed **2,815 species** across **51 targeted angiosperm clades** selected for animal-mediated pollination and fleshy-fruit dispersal, with a cleaned **6,169,815** GBIF occurrences and **4,757,769** spatially thinned species–site records. They categorised flowers into eight human-visible hues, used independent trait scoring derived from images/species descriptions rather than the Mois ROI-v4 labels, and evaluated WorldClim mean temperature, **aridity index** (precipitation/potential evapotranspiration) and UV-B exposure. Clade was a model grouping factor for multinomial colour regressions; the paper also investigated assemblage-level Shannon diversity. The climatic geography used independently filtered species-range GBIF sites, not the specific Mois source photo-site environmental join.

**What this study actually supports:** Purple flowers increased with aridity; yellow flowers were concentrated in dry warm habitats; white flowers were the most common category in the sampled assemblages, but their relative representation declined under several stress gradients. This provides a **qualitatively compatible published precedent** for a wet-associated photographic-white trend, not validation of the Mois original BIO12 +2.255 pp/source SD estimate. The other paper's multicolour multinomial log-odds relative to white, clade selection, aridity index and phylogenetic grouping are not directly commensurable with Mois's four-colour genus-centred linear photo-white BIO12.

**An especially important external measurement warning:** Dellinger et al. report about **82% white** in the more inclusive light-colour-as-white coding, but only approximately **50% or 55%** under the reported alternate binning schemes on their own sampled species (Figure 3 and Appendix S1). These are **reported author estimates**, not percentages independently recomputed here; the cohorts, denominators and categorisation definitions differ from Mois's **46.95%** white among 45,953 classifiable photograph labels. That independently reported large white-frequency swing supplies a specific competing explanation for apparent near 50:50 *without any colour-morph fitness balancing*. The Dellinger study also used some GBIF/iNaturalist flower images for manual colour scoring; different labelling does not mean independently sampled visual media in every case.

**Newer external clade source:** Sinnott-Armstrong, Maier, Smith & Dellinger (2025) released Dryad DOI [10.5061/dryad.r4xgxd2sc](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.r4xgxd2sc), with `final_dataset.csv`, 51 clade phylogenies and flower/fruit hues for **2,960 species**. The accompanying study appeared as an _American Journal of Botany_ article (2026), DOI [10.1002/ajb2.70146](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajb2.70146). This is a **related lab/clade sampling programme**, not an independent replicate of Dellinger et al.; it can nevertheless be **analytically independent of Mois photo-colour labels** if imported and processed separately. Do not use that CSV to claim the 2,815-species geographic/aridity result: the 2,960-species file contains `clade`, `species`, `flower_color`, `fruit_color` but no independently linked BIO12 or georeferenced occurrences in its published data description.

## Independent external white-colour clustering — executed on actual species records

**Input provenance and scope.** A saved Library file named `final_dataset.csv` was found and copied into an authorized working environment. Its byte size is **118,867**, SHA256 **`a253308785e4cbd0e361b3ca04cdfdae29c523eefa843375cebf8030ee0874af`**. The columns (`clade,species,flower_color,fruit_color`), exactly **2,960 unique species**, **51 clades**, and actual eight visible flower labels (`black, green, orange, pink, purple, red, white, yellow`) agree with the Dryad repository's public source description. This is **not cryptographic verification against a publisher-side manifest SHA**, which was unavailable during this audit. No source species/clade row data were committed to public Mois. The study and provenance are independently citable as Dryad DOI `10.5061/dryad.r4xgxd2sc`.

**Original external species-level white-colour census:** exactly **1,449/2,960 = 48.9527%** white in the released CSV, while the equally weighted mean of the **51** clade white shares is **48.1939%**. There are no source-missing flower colour cells. This nearly-half on a separate curated dataset is **not** evidence of white/coloured stable genetic morph frequency or globally representative floral sampling. Mois's source photographic white share **46.948%** has different observational denominators and classification rules.

**Prespecified, now executed white-colour clustering diagnostic.** Let `y_i=1` only for an explicit `flower_color == white`, and `y_i=0` for all seven other *actual* released hues; do not reclassify pale colours or treat `black` as `black/dark` or `purple` as `blue/purple` implicitly. For sampled clades with at least 3 taxa, calculate the fraction of binary-white total sum of squares explained by clade mean composition, `eta² = Σ_g n_g (p_g−p)²/[N p (1−p)]`. Independently permute the fixed 2,960 binary labels across all original clade sizes **9,999** times with seed `202610152328`; this tests a limited *colour-label exchangeability across the observed clades* null, not a phylogenetic evolutionary model, independent species samples or worldwide probability distribution.

A separate **9,999**-draw resampling of the **51 selected clades** (seed `202610152329`) gave an illustrative **40.93–58.41%** range for species-weighted white fraction and **17.39–39.41%** for the clade-mean eta². These are **conditional, descriptive clade-resampling intervals**; the source intentionally selected animal-pollinated and fleshy-fruited lineages rather than drawing random clades from worldwide angiosperms. There is no basis to interpret their nominal 95% coverage as uncertainty for all global flora.

**Interpretation:** A global approximately 50:50 white–nonwhite labelled *species total* can hide substantial clade-specific near-fixation of a visible category. This supports **taxonomic trait clustering under an independent human-curated classification**, consonant with but **not numerically commensurable** with Mois's earlier **34.38% variance by nominal genus, versus 9.33% size-permutation reference** (20,725 photo-classifiable taxa; different source, group definitions, sampling, classifier and null). The published 51 clades have no species-locality BIO12 or vapour data in this four-column file; **no independent climate–colour coefficient, flower pigment cost, pollinator benefit, fitness trade-off, morph genetic equilibrium, or genus-holdout pairwise prediction has been established**.

## Exploratory nominal-genus sensitivity: does the source's 51-clade grouping cause the result?

Actual external species names have **118 distinct initial genus tokens**; in **159/2,960** source rows this token differs from the dataset's broader `clade` label. Retaining nominal genera with ≥3 source taxa gives **2,902 species in 68 groups**. White frequency is **49.14%** species-equal but **43.79%** under equally weighted nominal genus means. Between-nominal-genus binary-white variance is **28.565%** versus **2.309%** for **9,999** genus-size-preserving permutations (seed `202610152330`, Monte Carlo `P=0.0001`), with **19/68=27.94%** nearly all-white or all-nonwhite genera versus **3.48%** null mean. The original source's 51-clade grouping explains **27.969%** on the **same 2,902 species**, so the broad group design does not create the descriptive clustering result by itself. These name-derived nominal genera are **not** phylogenetically authenticated, and this was an **exploratory sensitivity after the first external clade result**, not a separately prespecified or independent ecological sample. Original source species rows are not deposited; [safe aggregate receipt](../results/mois_external_nominal_genus_hue_20261015.json) and [name-grouping replayer](../scripts/analysis/mois_external_nominal_genus_sensitivity_20261015.py) permit audit with an authorized copy of the same external original CSV.

## Implication for GEB submission

The introduction now cites Dellinger et al. as a genuine **global** flower-colour environment precedent, and Discussion 4.1 explicitly avoids claiming novelty in the simple wet/white sign. Retain as differentiators: source-photo denominator transparency; species-vs-genus scale contrast on the same original taxa; original genus contribution heterogeneity; exact original maxT and matched-local negative controls; out-of-genus pairwise predictions and their limits; documented 54.3% unclassified photos. Do **not** equate the newly observed **27.48% external clade composition** with Mois's **34.38% photographed nominal-genus composition**, or state that the external source replicates Mois's rainfall slope. It confirms a **qualitatively concordant taxonomic clustering pattern under distinct species-colour labels only**. Pigment production, pollination, pollen/seed fitness and selection remain unmeasured.
